The theory, in full

How DermaSense reaches a number

00The reframe: an active and its carrier, not three rival compounds

The instinct is to output "X% pulcherrimin, Y% xylolipid, Z% lyso-ornithine" — three numbers that sum to a formula. That quietly conflates a drug with its delivery vehicle. Pulcherrimin is the active: it absorbs UV and scavenges reactive oxygen species (ROS). Xylolipid and lyso-ornithine are biosurfactants — they spread and deliver the active, they don't protect the skin themselves. So DermaSense makes two coupled but separate decisions: how much active (driven by the environment) and what carrier blend (driven by the skin). This split is what lets every coefficient be defended individually instead of a black box emitting three correlated guesses.

01Environment → oxidative load measured · validated

Rather than invent pollutant weights, DermaSense uses Canada's Air Quality Health Index (AQHI) — a peer-reviewed, government-adopted index whose coefficients are excess-mortality relative risks fitted to multi-city daily time-series data (Stieb et al., 2008). It is additive across pollutants and has no lower threshold, exactly the behaviour we want for "cumulative oxidative burden".

# NO₂ and O₃ are converted µg/m³ → ppb (×0.531, ×0.509 at 25 °C) before entering the formula
AQHI = (1000/10.4) · [ (e^0.000537·O₃ − 1) + (e^0.000871·NO₂ − 1) + (e^0.000487·PM2.5 − 1) ]

AQHI captures pollution but not sunlight, and UV is a primary driver of cutaneous ROS and antioxidant depletion. So the console adds the WHO/WMO UV Index (itself a validated erythemal quantity) as a second term, and reports a single 0–100 oxidative load. The 60/40 emphasis between the two is the one modelling choice here — it is labelled adjustable, and both inputs underneath it are real standardised indices.

load = 100 · ( 0.60·clamp(AQHI/10, 0, 1) + 0.40·clamp(UV/11, 0, 1) )

02Load → pulcherrimin dose measured anchors

Pulcherrimin is an iron(III)-chelating cyclic dipeptide pigment from Metschnikowia yeast. Crucially, a 2024 study measured its behaviour on human HaCaT keratinocytes (Kregiel et al.), which gives real anchors instead of assumptions:

# measured (Kregiel 2024)
0.20 mg/mL  → SPF 20        (effective floor)
0.40 mg/mL  → 26% ROS↓ @5 mM H₂O₂
1.58 mg/mL  → highest non-cytotoxic
3.20 mg/mL  → viability drops to 84%
# dose rule (mg/mL)
form_max = 1.00 − 0.40·(S/100)
dose = clamp(0.20 + (form_max−0.20)·load/100,
             0.20, form_max)

The floor is the concentration that actually delivered SPF 20; below it there is no meaningful photoprotection. The hard ceiling is the highest concentration that left keratinocyte viability intact. The working formulation maximum sits at 1.00 mg/mL — a ≈1.6× uncertainty factor below that measured ceiling, standard toxicology practice — and is pulled lower still for sensitive skin. Every recommendation is reported with its safety margin (how many-fold below the cytotoxic concentration it sits). The load-to-dose interpolation is linear because the measured ROS data give two points, not a curve; refining that shape is a first-order job for the calibration loop.

03Skin → carrier blend direction real · gains to-fit

The two biosurfactants sit at different points on the hydrophilic–lipophilic balance (HLB) scale, the classic Griffin framework for matching a surfactant to a job. Glycolipids such as xylolipid are relatively hydrophilic (higher HLB) — better at spreading and cleansing, which suits oily skin and sebum-rich surfaces. Amino-acid-derived lipids such as lyso-ornithine are milder and more barrier-compatible (lower effective HLB), which suits dry / sensitive skin, where high-HLB surfactants are more likely to strip and irritate.

xylo_frac = clamp( 0.50 + 0.25·(O−50)/50 − 0.25·(S−50)/50 , 0.15 , 0.85 )   # O = oiliness, S = sensitivity

The direction of this rule is grounded in surfactant science and cited literature; the magnitude of the gains is honestly a placeholder, to be fitted by tensiometry (measured HLB of the team's own compounds) and trans-epidermal water loss (TEWL). This is the weakest-anchored of the three decisions, and the console says so rather than dressing it up.

Reading skin from a photo

What the image analysis actually measures

The photo mode does not use machine learning or send your image anywhere — it runs a transparent, inspectable analysis of the pixels in your browser (HTML Canvas). You can click a specific patch of skin to sample just that spot. It derives the same two skin axes the questionnaire produces:

Oiliness ← specular shine. Oily skin reflects light as bright, low-saturation glare. The analyser counts the fraction of skin pixels that are near-white and desaturated (L>210, sat<0.25) and maps that glare fraction onto the oiliness axis.

Sensitivity ← erythema (a*). The pixels are converted sRGB → CIE-Lab; the a* channel is the green–red axis, a standard proxy for skin redness / erythema. Higher a* nudges the sensitivity axis up.

Skin tone ← ITA°. The Individual Typology Angle, atan((L*−50)/b*), is the dermatology-standard tone measure (very light > 55° … dark < −30°). It gives a Fitzpatrick-like hint that lighter skin tends to burn more easily, and — importantly — it flags when to distrust the redness reading.

Known limitation. Optical erythema is genuinely harder to detect on deeper skin tones, a well-documented bias in skin imaging. That is why photo mode is a cross-check, never the sole input, and why the ITA° and a caution are shown alongside every result.

Scope & safety

Research prototype — no medical claims. There is no published clinical dose-response literature for these topical compound ratios. DermaSense is a testable hypothesis to be calibrated against bench data, not a validated clinical tool, and makes no diagnostic claim.

Two honest caveats worth stating up front: (1) The measured dose anchors are in-vitro (keratinocyte and spectrophotometric) concentrations; translating an in-vitro effective concentration to a final topical cream still requires the team's own permeation and patch-test work. (2) The photo analyser is an optical proxy with a documented deeper-skin-tone bias, offered as a cross-check to the questionnaire — never as a stand-alone verdict.

Calibration roadmap (Phase 4). Each to-fit coefficient maps to one bench assay: dose curve ← DPPH/ABTS vs concentration on the team's own pulcherrimin; formulation ceiling ← keratinocyte cytotoxicity on the final extract; blend gains ← tensiometry (HLB) + TEWL; air-vs-UV weight ← the team's own protection assays under combined challenge.
